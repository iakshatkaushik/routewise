    Chapter 05

    A big model taught the small one.

    The big model (the teacher) first learns the task. It then gives a probability for every training prompt, using a copy of itself that never saw that prompt. The small model (the student) learns from those probabilities and from the real labels. The labels are a single judge's grade, so they are noisy; the teacher's probabilities are smoother.

    Teacher and student, on one line

    Validation AUC. A stronger teacher stopped helping: the small student became the limit.

    Some kinds of prompts fail far more often.

    K-Means grouped the training prompts by their words, without looking at the labels. Each bar is one group, named by its most typical words. The dashed line is the average failure rate. Coral groups fail more often than average, mint groups less.

    Chapter 08

    Patterns that predict a failure.

    Apriori, written from scratch, searched for combinations of prompt properties that come before a failure. Lift says how many times more likely a failure is when the rule matches, compared with the average.

    Apriori against the naive method

    Both find the same frequent itemsets. Apriori skips candidates that cannot be frequent.

    Chapter 09

    Predicting the score itself.

    Instead of pass or fail, these models predict the cheap model's 1 to 5 score. A lower predicted score means a riskier prompt, so each model can also be used as a router.
