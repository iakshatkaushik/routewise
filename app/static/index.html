How it works

From prompt to decision in a few milliseconds.

No LLM is involved in the decision. The router is classical machine learning trained on real prompts whose cheap-model answers were graded 1-5 by a judge. It learns, from the prompt alone, which ones the cheap model is likely to get wrong.

01

Prompt

Your text, up to 4,000 characters, exactly as typed. Nothing is sent to an LLM yet.

02

Features

The text becomes one long numeric vector.

  • word TF-IDF: words and word pairs
  • character TF-IDF: short character pieces
  • prompt statistics: length, code, maths, constraints…
03

Classifiers

Each component model scores the prompt on its own.

  • loading…
04

Stacker

A logistic-regression stacker merges the component scores into one calibrated probability: P(cheap fails).

05

Threshold

Your budget sets the cut-off. At 30%, the threshold is the risk level that the riskiest 30% of validation prompts exceed.

06

Decision

One call to one model.

below → cheap LLM above → strong LLM

Why not always use the strong model?

Strong models cost far more per call and are slower. Most everyday prompts don't need them, so paying for every call wastes money.

What is "quality gap recovered"?

If cheap-only scores Q0 and strong-only scores Q1, a router that reaches Q recovers (Q − Q0) ÷ (Q1 − Q0) of the gap. A random router recovers it in proportion to the calls it spends.

Where do the reasons come from?

From the weights of a linear model trained on the same features: each word, character piece or statistic in your prompt pushes the risk up or down. It is a readable approximation, not the full stacked model.
